- Commitment Target: In next Tuesday's Science lesson with Year 4 after school holidays. I will focus on upgrading the peer talk element. Two students who rarely volunteer ended up offering really thoughtful insights because they finally had the time to process the question.

### [2026-05-18] Teacher Reflection: Habib
- Date: May 18, 2026
- Confidence (1-5): Blank
- PD Focus / Level: Dialogic Practice / Developing
- Strategies: Wait time, Cold call, Talk partners, Sentence frames, Vocabulary display, Questioning
- What Was Noticed: Dialogic teaching - I tried the post question, give 1 minute for the pupils to discuss in their group, then cold call the lest confident pupil in each group randomly. This encourage all the group members to participate in the discussion and one unofficial leader emerge from this. The weaker pupils tend to follow what the leader said, sometimes they even voiced their own idea regardless of what the group agreed upon. One or two pupils did not really contribute ideas but just listen to the whole discussion, sometimes got distracted and drift from the activity and start doing something else that's not related to the activity or topic. Apart from all the minor setback, I noticed more English terms are used in group discussion, although they still mainly use Malay or sometimes Iban. As of today, 3 pupils out of 26 had tried to response fully in English. From my perspective, I think they are still not confident enough to response in English even though some of them already know quite a few English terms when it comes to the topic of fractions, reason not determine yet. *walkie-talkie became the tool to encourage some pupils to response.
- Commitment Target: Next lesson will be on addition of two fractions with the same denominator. The plan is: Shorten my questions to the basic understandable sentence, meaning use less "or" in a sentence. example: "Are the denominators in both fraction the same?" instead of: "Is the numerator larger than || or smaller than || or the same as the other denominator?" Wait time increase to 90secs instead of 60secs when using the "Why" card. Wait time for "What card" remains 60secs. Brief pause (<10s) will be implemented after an explanation, before explaining another concept or skill or operation. Fan of Control - breath in and breath out mode. Example: Identify if both fractions have the same denominator -> can perform addition or not -> if can, how to perform it. We-do part needs to have a simple worksheet, to help them see what the actual skill or operation is done (cognitive theory: giving complete solution in written form before the you-do part).
- Internal Shift Observed: Script is a distraction, the class became stiff and I found it hard to focus on the pupils reactions. A few pupils drift and didn't take part in activities - possibly because the lesson became too relax as they can say out the groups collective answers while they themself might not have a solid understanding on the topic discussed. Confident level in using mixed English terms when responding during cold call is noticeable still low, even though in group discussion they are using the terms in English. Dominant pupil or leader in each groups tend to lead the others to respond based on what they understand.
- Question for Coach: Rephrase and restructure the pupils answers or responses in English, and then get confirmation of what they are actually trying to say. I always gets the "yes" or a nod from the pupils that are responding. How much true the rephrase and restructure is, no proof to back it up. For cold call, I always starts with the weakest group to avoid those groups to copy the dominant group's answer or response. Most of the time, they gave the correct answer and same as the dominant group. As for the pause time during discussion, I didn't leave my post in front of the pupils, observing from the front didn't give me what actually happened in each groups but it gave me a rough idea of what they are doing, either discussing the answer or explaining to each other or persuading all the members to agree with one or two dominant pupil in the group.
